Kick a ball, and it flies across the playground. Switch on a lamp, and the room lights up. Eat your
breakfast, and you have the strength to run and play. Behind all of this is one and the same thing:
**energy**.

## What is energy?

Energy is what makes things happen. Whenever something starts to move, warms up, lights up or makes
a sound, energy is at work.

Energy is not a thing you can hold, like a stone or a cup of water. But you can always see what it
does. Things move, get warm, shine or make a noise.

## The many forms of energy

Energy can hide in many different forms. Here are the most important ones.

| Form of energy | Where you can find it |
|---|---|
| Movement | a rolling ball, the wind, you riding a bike |
| Stored in height | a book on a high shelf, you at the top of a slide |
| Stored in stretching | a stretched rubber band, the spring in a wind-up toy |
| Stored in food and fuel | bread, apples, wood, petrol, batteries |
| Heat | a warm radiator, hot soup |
| Light | sunshine, a torch |
| Sound | a drum, a voice, a barking dog |
| Electrical | electricity flowing through wires |

Everything that moves has movement energy. The heavier and faster it is, the more energy it has. A
truck rolling down a street has far more than a marble rolling at the same speed.

Stored energy is energy that waits until it is needed. Lift a ball up high, and it gets stored
energy, ready to turn into movement when you let go. A stretched rubber band stores energy too.
Release it, and it snaps back.

Food and fuel are full of stored energy as well. It stays locked inside until your body, a fire or
an engine sets it free. [[heat|Heat]] and light are energy too, and so is the electricity that flows
through wires as [[electric-current|electric current]].

<Analogy>
Energy is a bit like water. Water can be hard ice, runny water or invisible steam. It looks
completely different each time, but it is still the same water. Energy is like that too: movement,
heat and light look nothing alike, yet they are all energy.
</Analogy>

## Energy changes form

Energy loves to change from one form into another. Look around, and you will see it happening
everywhere:

- A lamp turns electrical energy into light, and a little heat.
- A toaster turns electrical energy into heat.
- On a slide, your height turns into speed.
- A car engine turns the energy stored in fuel into movement, and a lot of heat.
- A drum turns the movement of your hands into sound.

However it changes, energy never disappears. It only moves from place to place or turns into another
form. This is one of the most important rules in all of science. Read more in
[[conservation-of-energy|why energy never disappears]].

## A long journey: from the Sun to your legs

Where does the energy in your body come from? Let's follow it on its long journey.

The Sun sends light down to Earth. Plants catch that light with their leaves. They use its energy to
make sugar, and the energy stays stored inside the plant.

Then you eat the plant, as bread, fruit or vegetables. Or you eat food from an animal that ate
plants. Your body sets the stored energy free, and your muscles use it to run and jump.

Running makes you warm. In the end, the energy spreads out into the air around you as heat.

Most of the energy we use on Earth began its journey in the Sun. That includes the food we eat and
the wood we burn. Even the wind blows because the Sun warms some places more than others.

<FunFact>
Coal, oil and natural gas formed from plants and tiny sea creatures that lived millions of years ago.
So a car running on petrol is really using sunlight that reached Earth long, long ago. Some of it
arrived even before the time of the dinosaurs.
</FunFact>

## How do we measure energy?

Scientists measure energy in **joules** (say "jools"). They write **J** for short. The name honours
James Prescott Joule, an English scientist who studied heat and energy about 180 years ago.

One joule is a small amount. It is about the energy you need to lift a small apple from the floor up
to a door handle. Because a joule is so small, we often count in **kilojoules** (kJ). One kilojoule
is 1,000 joules.

Look at a food packet. It shows how much energy the food contains, in kilojoules (kJ) and in
**kilocalories** (kcal). People often shorten kilocalories to "calories". One kilocalorie is about
4,200 joules.

A banana holds about 400 kJ, or about 100 kcal. That is as much energy as lifting a small apple up to
a door handle 400,000 times!

<GoDeeper title="Movement energy in numbers">
The energy of a moving object is called **kinetic energy**:

$$
E = \frac{1}{2} \cdot m \cdot v^2
$$

Here $E$ is the energy in joules (J), $m$ is the mass in kilograms (kg) and $v$ is the speed in
metres per second (m/s).

A child with a mass of 30 kg running at 4 m/s (about 14 km/h) has
$\frac{1}{2} \cdot 30 \cdot 4^2 = 240$ joules of movement energy.

Because the speed is squared, twice the speed means four times the energy. That is why a fast car is
so much more dangerous than a slow one: it has far more energy to get rid of in a crash.

Energy stored by height (**gravitational potential energy**) is $E = m \cdot g \cdot h$. Here $h$ is
the height in metres and $g \approx 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$ tells how strongly the Earth pulls. A 0.1 kg
apple lifted by 1 m gets $0.1 \cdot 9.8 \cdot 1 \approx 1$ joule: the apple from the text.
</GoDeeper>

<GoDeeper title="Energy and power are not the same">
Energy tells *how much*. [[electric-power|Power]] tells *how fast*: how much energy is moved or
changed every second. Power is measured in watts (W), and one watt is one joule per second:

$$
1\ \text{W} = 1\ \frac{\text{J}}{\text{s}}
$$

A 10 W LED lamp uses 10 joules every second. A 2,000 W kettle uses 2,000 joules every second, 200
times as fast.

Electricity bills count energy in kilowatt-hours (kWh). One kilowatt-hour is 1,000 watts for one
hour: $1000 \cdot 3600 = 3\,600\,000$ joules.
</GoDeeper>

<GoDeeper title="Where the picture of energy forms breaks down">
- Energy is not a substance like water. It is a quantity that can be calculated for any situation,
  and nature keeps its total the same.
- The list of forms is a handy way to sort energy, but several forms are the same thing in disguise.
  Heat is the movement energy of tiny jiggling particles, and sound is air moving back and forth.
  Energy in food and fuel is stored in the way [[atom|atoms]] are joined together.
- Most forms belong to two big families: **kinetic energy** (energy of movement) and **potential
  energy** (stored energy). Light is special: it carries energy on its own, even through empty
  space.
- The Sun's own energy comes from its centre, where hydrogen is squeezed together into helium. This
  is called **nuclear fusion**.
</GoDeeper>

<Remember>
- Energy is what makes things happen: movement, warmth, light, sound and growth.
- It comes in many forms: movement, stored energy, heat, light, sound and electrical energy.
- Energy changes from one form into another, but it never disappears.
- Most of the energy we use started out as sunlight.
- We measure energy in joules (J). Food packets also use kilocalories (kcal).
</Remember>
