Look around you: a wooden table, a glass of water, the air you breathe, your own hand. They could
hardly be more different. Yet they are all built in the same way — from tiny building blocks called
**atoms**.

## What happens if you keep cutting?

Imagine a small piece of gold. Cut it in half. Then cut one of the halves in half again, and again,
and again. Each new piece is smaller, but it is still gold.

Can you go on like this forever? No! In the end you reach the smallest bit of gold there is: a
single gold **atom**. It is made of even smaller parts, but none of those parts is gold on its own.

No knife is sharp enough to really do this. But atoms are real. With special microscopes,
scientists can even see them as tiny, blurry bumps.

<FunFact>
The word “atom” comes from an ancient Greek word that means “uncuttable”. About 2,400 years ago, a
Greek thinker named Democritus guessed that everything is made of tiny pieces that cannot be cut.
He was right about the tiny pieces. Today we know that even atoms have smaller parts inside.
</FunFact>

## How small is an atom?

Atoms are much smaller than anything you have ever seen. Not even a good school microscope can
show them.

Look at one of your hairs and see how thin it is. To reach across it, you would need to line up
several hundred thousand atoms side by side.

<Analogy>
Imagine blowing up an apple until it is as big as the whole Earth. The atoms inside it would then
be only about as big as cherries.
</Analogy>

<FunFact>
Your body is built from about 7 billion billion billion atoms. That is a 7 with 27 zeros after it!
</FunFact>

## What is inside an atom?

An atom has a middle and an outside. The middle is called the **nucleus**. It is tiny, but it
weighs almost as much as the whole atom.

The nucleus is made of two kinds of particles, packed tightly together:

- **Protons** carry a plus [[electric-charge|electric charge]].
- **Neutrons** carry no charge at all. They are neutral, which is how they got their name.

Around the nucleus move much lighter particles called [[electron|electrons]]. They carry a minus
charge. Plus and minus pull on each other, and this pull keeps the electrons close to the nucleus.
The electrons move in a fuzzy cloud, called the **electron cloud**, arranged in layers called
[[electron-shell|electron shells]].

A normal atom has exactly as many electrons as protons. The plus and the minus cancel out, so the
atom as a whole has no charge. An atom that has lost or gained electrons does have a charge, and it
is called an [[ion]].

<GoDeeper title="A fuzzy cloud, not tiny planets">
Many pictures show electrons circling the nucleus like planets around the Sun. Real electrons do
not follow neat paths like that. Quantum physics can only tell where an electron is *likely* to
be. These likely places form a fuzzy cloud around the nucleus, called an **orbital**. Orbitals come
in different sizes and shapes, and they decide how an atom joins with other atoms.

Protons and neutrons are made of even smaller particles called quarks. Electrons, as far as anyone
knows, are not made of anything smaller.
</GoDeeper>

## An atom is mostly empty

<Analogy>
Imagine an atom as big as a huge football stadium. The nucleus would be about the size of a pea in
the middle. The electrons would be somewhere in a fuzzy cloud reaching up to the highest seats.
Everything else would be empty space.
</Analogy>

So why can’t your hand pass straight through a table? The electrons on the outside of your hand’s
atoms push against the electrons of the table’s atoms. Minus pushes minus away, so your hand stops
at the surface.

<GoDeeper title="How small, exactly?">
- Atoms are roughly 0.1 to 0.5 nanometres across. A nanometre is a millionth of a millimetre.
- A human hair is between about 0.02 and 0.18 millimetres thick. So several hundred thousand atoms
  fit across a typical hair.
- The nucleus is tens of thousands of times smaller across than the whole atom: about 27,000 times
  for uranium and about 60,000 times for hydrogen. Books often round this to “100,000 times”.
- Yet the nucleus holds more than 99.9% of the atom’s mass. A proton weighs about 1,836 times as
  much as an electron, and a neutron a little more.
- What really stops your hand at the table is the electric push between electrons, together with a
  quantum rule called the Pauli exclusion principle. It stops electrons from crowding into the
  same state.
</GoDeeper>

## How many kinds of atoms are there?

Gold atoms, oxygen atoms, iron atoms… Scientists have found 118 different kinds of atoms so far.
Each kind is called a [[chemical-element|chemical element]].

What makes one element different from another? The number of protons in the nucleus. Every
hydrogen atom has 1 proton, every carbon atom has 6, every oxygen atom has 8 and every gold atom
has 79.

The number of neutrons, though, can differ a little, even between atoms of the same element. These
versions of an element are called [[isotope|isotopes]].

More than 90 elements are found in nature. The others were made by scientists in laboratories. All
of them are listed in a famous chart called the [[periodic-table|periodic table]].

## What do atoms do?

Atoms often hold on to each other with [[chemical-bond|chemical bonds]]. A group of atoms stuck
together is called a [[molecule]]. The smallest bit of water, for example, is a molecule made of two
hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom.

Most things around you are built from only a handful of kinds of atoms, joined in countless ways.
It is like building a whole town from a box that holds only a few kinds of bricks.

Atoms never sit still, either. They jiggle and bump into each other all the time. The warmer
something is, the faster its atoms jiggle — and that is what [[temperature]] tells us.

<FunFact>
Atoms are very old. The carbon and oxygen atoms in your body were made inside stars, billions of
years ago — long before the Earth existed.
</FunFact>

<Remember>
- All the stuff around you is built from tiny atoms, far too small to see.
- An atom has a tiny, heavy nucleus made of protons and neutrons, with light electrons around it.
- Protons carry a plus charge, electrons a minus charge, and neutrons no charge.
- An atom is mostly empty space.
- There are 118 known kinds of atoms, called elements.
</Remember>
